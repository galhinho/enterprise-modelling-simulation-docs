# Business Model

Status: 📝 Draft

## Definition

Business is the coordinated system through which an enterprise pursues, creates, delivers, preserves, captures, or distributes value.

Business is not limited to commercial activity. Public-service, educational, research, charitable, healthcare, and other enterprises also organize capabilities and activities around intended value.

## Purpose

The Business model translates value objectives into the capabilities and coordinated activity required to pursue them.

It helps answer:

- what the enterprise is trying to achieve;
- which stakeholders and outcomes matter;
- what the enterprise must be able to do;
- which requirements follow from those capabilities;
- how supporting domains contribute to business outcomes.

## Business capabilities

A business capability describes an ability the enterprise requires, independent of the specific organization, person, process, or technology used to realize it.

Capabilities bridge business objectives and enterprise implementation. They may create requirements for:

- organizational structures and coordination;
- responsibilities and authority;
- people, skills, and experience;
- participation and identity;
- knowledge creation and transfer;
- technology resources and services.

## Principles

- Business context drives enterprise structure.
- Capabilities describe what is needed before prescribing how it is implemented.
- Supporting domains enable capabilities without becoming the definition of the business.
- Business decisions and trade-offs should remain explainable through value objectives.

## Boundaries

This model defines business concepts and relationships. Detailed operating procedures, process execution, commercial plans, and private generation rules are outside its public scope.
