# Organizational Characteristics

Status: 📝 Draft

---

# Purpose

This document defines the concept of Organizational Characteristics within the Enterprise Modelling & Simulation.

Organizational Characteristics represent tendencies, priorities, and orientations that influence how an organization operates, makes decisions, and implements capabilities.

Characteristics help explain why organizations with similar structures and capabilities may behave differently.

---

# Overview

Organizations are not defined solely by their structures, responsibilities, or participants.

Organizations may exhibit differing priorities, preferences, and decision-making approaches that influence enterprise outcomes.

These characteristics may affect:

- Capability implementation
- Organizational design
- Technology adoption
- Risk management
- Investment decisions
- Governance approaches
- Enterprise evolution

As a result, organizations with similar structures may produce significantly different outcomes.

---

# Characteristics as Spectrums

Organizational Characteristics should not be treated as binary classifications.

For example:

```text
Innovative = True
Conservative = False
```

is often insufficient.

Instead, characteristics should be viewed as spectrums that may vary in strength.

Examples:

```text
Innovation Orientation
0 ─────────────────────── 1000

Risk Tolerance
0 ─────────────────────── 1000

Environmental Orientation
0 ─────────────────────── 1000
```

Organizations may simultaneously exhibit multiple characteristics with varying strengths.

---

# Examples

Potential characteristics may include:

- Innovation Orientation
- Risk Tolerance
- Compliance Focus
- Cost Sensitivity
- Growth Orientation
- Operational Discipline
- Decision Speed
- Security Focus
- Technology Adoption
- Environmental Orientation
- People Orientation

This list is intentionally incomplete and may evolve over time.

---

# Relationship to Capabilities

Capabilities define what an organization must be able to do.

Characteristics may influence how those capabilities are implemented.

For example:

Capability:

```text
Software Development
```

May be implemented differently by:

```text
Highly Innovative Organization
```

versus

```text
Highly Conservative Organization
```

despite sharing the same capability.

Characteristics therefore influence implementation decisions without changing the underlying capability itself.

---

# Relationship to Enterprise Generation

Characteristics may influence enterprise generation.

Examples may include:

- Organizational structure preferences
- Hiring priorities
- Technology selection tendencies
- Governance approaches
- Risk management strategies
- Investment decisions

Characteristics help shape the initial form of an enterprise.

---

# Architectural Observation

Capabilities influence what an organization requires.

Characteristics influence how an organization tends to respond to those requirements.

Together, Capabilities and Characteristics may significantly influence enterprise design and decision-making.

---

# Future Considerations

Future simulator evolution may introduce concepts related to organizational change and adaptation.

Examples may include:

- Adaptability
- Change Resistance
- Cultural Inertia
- Learning Capacity

These concepts are related to organizational evolution and should not be considered equivalent to Organizational Characteristics.
