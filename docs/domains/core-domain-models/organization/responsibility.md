# Responsibility

Status: 📝 Draft

---

## Overview

Responsibility is a foundational concept within the Organization Domain.

Organizations exist, in part, to allocate, coordinate, govern, and execute responsibilities required to support business objectives.

Responsibilities provide the connection between what the enterprise must achieve and how accountability for achieving it is distributed.

---

## Definition

A Responsibility represents an obligation to ensure that a specific outcome, activity, capability, resource, or decision is managed appropriately.

Responsibility does not necessarily mean that the responsible party performs all related work directly.

Instead, it identifies who or what is expected to ensure that the work is completed, governed, or coordinated.

---

## Purpose

The purpose of Responsibility within the Organization Domain is to establish clear expectations regarding:

- What must be done.
- Who is expected to ensure it is done.
- Who has authority to make related decisions.
- Who is accountable for the outcome.
- How work is coordinated across the organization.

---

## Characteristics

Responsibilities:

- Exist within an organizational context.
- Support business objectives and capabilities.
- May be assigned to people, roles, teams, departments, or organizational units.
- May be delegated without removing accountability.
- May change as the organization evolves.
- May overlap or depend upon other responsibilities.
- May require authority, resources, and access to be fulfilled.

---

## Core Relationships

### Capability

Capabilities describe what the enterprise must be able to do.

Responsibilities describe who or what is accountable for ensuring that those capabilities are delivered.

Capabilities and Responsibilities are related, but neither should be treated as a direct child of the other.

---

### Role

Roles group related responsibilities into a reusable organizational function.

A role may contain multiple responsibilities, and the same responsibility may appear across different roles depending on organizational structure.

---

### Authority

Authority defines the permitted scope of decision-making associated with a responsibility.

Responsibility without sufficient authority may be impossible to fulfill effectively.

---

### Accountability

Accountability identifies who is ultimately answerable for the result.

Responsibility may be shared or delegated, while accountability should remain clearly identifiable.

---

### Ownership

Ownership represents sustained responsibility for an entity, resource, capability, process, or outcome.

Ownership is one mechanism through which responsibility is expressed.

---

### People

People perform work and may be assigned responsibilities directly or through roles and positions.

---

### Organization

The Organization Domain allocates, coordinates, and governs responsibilities.

Organizational structures such as teams and departments emerge partly from the need to manage related responsibilities.

## Invariants

The following conditions should remain true:

- Every responsibility must have a defined scope.
- Every responsibility must have an identifiable accountable subject.
- Responsibilities should support an organizational or business objective.
- A responsibility should not be assigned without sufficient authority and resources.
- Delegation does not automatically remove accountability.
- Responsibilities may evolve as organizational needs change.

---

## Design Principles

### Clear Accountability

Every responsibility should have a clearly identifiable accountable subject.

---

### Responsibility Before Structure

Organizational structures should emerge from the need to allocate and coordinate responsibilities.

---

### Delegation Without Ambiguity

Responsibilities may be delegated, but delegation should not obscure accountability.

---

### Sufficient Authority

A subject assigned a responsibility should receive enough authority, access, and resources to fulfill it.

---

### Evolvability

Responsibilities should be capable of changing as the organization, business, and operating environment evolve.

---

## Examples

Examples of organizational responsibilities include:

- Approving privileged access.
- Managing customer support operations.
- Maintaining network availability.
- Ensuring payroll accuracy.
- Owning an enterprise application.
- Responding to security incidents.
- Maintaining regulatory compliance.
- Coordinating employee onboarding.
