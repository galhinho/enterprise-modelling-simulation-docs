# Knowledge

Status: 📝 Draft

---

# Purpose

This document defines the concept of Knowledge within the Enterprise Modelling & Simulation.

Knowledge represents information, understanding, expertise, insights, and organizational memory that exist within the enterprise.

Knowledge enables participation, decision-making, problem-solving, capability execution, and organizational continuity.

Knowledge may exist within people, documentation, processes, technologies, and other enterprise artifacts.

---

# Overview

Knowledge is a supporting enterprise domain.

Knowledge contributes to the successful execution of enterprise capabilities and activities.

Knowledge may be acquired, shared, transferred, retained, concentrated, distributed, or lost over time.

Organizations frequently depend upon knowledge to operate effectively and maintain resilience.

---

# Definition

Knowledge represents information, understanding, expertise, and insights that contribute to enterprise operation and value creation.

Knowledge may be:

- General
- Specialized
- Technical
- Operational
- Organizational
- Domain-Specific

Knowledge is distinct from experience, although experience may contribute to knowledge development.

---

# Types of Knowledge

## Explicit Knowledge

Explicit Knowledge represents knowledge that has been documented, recorded, or otherwise made available beyond a specific individual.

Examples may include:

- Policies
- Procedures
- Runbooks
- Documentation
- Architecture Diagrams
- Knowledge Bases

Explicit Knowledge is generally easier to transfer, retain, and distribute.

---

## Tacit Knowledge

Tacit Knowledge represents knowledge that primarily exists within individuals.

Examples may include:

- Judgment
- Intuition
- Expertise
- Informal Practices
- Relationships
- Experience-Based Insights

Tacit Knowledge is frequently more difficult to document, transfer, and retain.

---

## Forms of Knowledge

Knowledge may exist in many forms, including:

- Structural Knowledge
- Operational Knowledge
- Technical Knowledge
- Business Knowledge
- Institutional Knowledge

These categories are not exhaustive and may evolve as the simulator matures.

---

# Characteristics

Knowledge:

- May be acquired through learning, training, observation, and experience
- May be general or specialized
- May be shared between individuals and groups
- May be documented or undocumented
- May evolve over time
- May become outdated
- May be difficult to replace or transfer
- May contribute to organizational resilience or organizational risk
- May become concentrated within specific individuals or groups

---

# Relationships

## People

People may acquire, create, share, apply, and transfer knowledge.

Knowledge may influence an individual's effectiveness and ability to perform responsibilities.

---

## Experience

Experience may contribute to the acquisition, refinement, and application of knowledge.

Knowledge and Experience are related but distinct concepts.

---

## Influence

Knowledge may contribute to an individual's influence within the enterprise.

Specialized or critical knowledge may increase influence regardless of formal authority.

---

## Capabilities

Capabilities may require specific knowledge to operate effectively.

Knowledge may influence capability effectiveness, maturity, and resilience.

---

## Technology

Technology may store, distribute, preserve, and enable access to knowledge.

Documentation systems, knowledge bases, collaboration platforms, and repositories may serve as knowledge containers.

---

## Organization

Organizations may depend upon critical knowledge held by individuals, teams, technologies, or repositories.

Knowledge distribution may influence organizational resilience.

---

## Identity

Participation represented through Identity may require specific Knowledge to be performed effectively.

---

# Design Principles

## Knowledge is Distinct from Experience

Knowledge and Experience should remain separate concepts.

Individuals may possess knowledge without extensive experience and may possess experience without complete knowledge.

Neither concept should be assumed to fully represent the other.

---

## Knowledge May Be Shared

Knowledge may exist beyond a single individual and may be transferred through communication, collaboration, documentation, and training.

---

## Knowledge May Be Concentrated

Organizations may become dependent upon knowledge concentrated within a small number of individuals, teams, or repositories.

---

## Knowledge May Be Lost

Knowledge loss may occur through personnel departures, inadequate documentation, organizational change, or technology retirement.

---

# Architectural Observation

Knowledge may become a critical enterprise dependency.

Organizations frequently rely upon knowledge concentrated within specific individuals, teams, technologies, or repositories.

As a result, knowledge transfer, retention, concentration, and loss may significantly influence enterprise effectiveness, resilience, and continuity.
