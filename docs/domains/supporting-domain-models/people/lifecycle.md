# People Lifecycle

Status: 📝 Draft

---

## Overview

The People Lifecycle describes how an individual's participation within the enterprise evolves over time.

The lifecycle focuses on enterprise participation rather than the life of the individual.

A Person may move through multiple participation states throughout their relationship with the enterprise.

---

## Lifecycle Stages

### Potential Participant

An individual exists outside the enterprise but may become a future participant.

Examples:

- Applicant
- Candidate
- Prospective Contractor
- Potential Partner

---

### Selection

The enterprise evaluates whether the individual should participate.

Examples:

- Recruitment
- Interviews
- Background Verification
- Skills Assessment

---

### Onboarding

The individual is introduced into the enterprise.

Examples:

- Hiring
- Contractor Engagement
- Initial Assignments
- Identity Creation
- Access Provisioning

---

### Active Participation

The individual actively contributes to enterprise activities.

Examples:

- Performing Responsibilities
- Occupying Positions
- Participating in Teams
- Using Enterprise Systems

---

### Internal Evolution

The individual's participation changes while remaining within the enterprise.

Examples:

- Promotion
- Demotion
- Transfer
- Role Change
- Department Change
- Responsibility Changes
- Skill Development
- Knowledge Growth
- Experience Accumulation

---

### Temporary Absence

Participation is temporarily reduced or suspended.

Examples:

- Vacation
- Leave of Absence
- Medical Leave
- Sabbatical

---

### Offboarding

The individual's participation within the enterprise ends.

Examples:

- Resignation
- Termination
- Contract Completion
- Retirement

Identity, access, responsibilities, and organizational assignments should be reviewed and updated accordingly.

---

### Former Participant

The individual no longer actively participates in the enterprise.

Knowledge, relationships, and historical contributions may continue to affect the organization.

---

### Re-engagement

A former participant may return to the enterprise.

Examples:

- Rehire
- New Contract
- Advisory Role

---

## Participation Decisions

Changes in enterprise participation are not always determined solely by formal qualifications, performance measurements, or organizational requirements.

Decisions involving hiring, promotion, demotion, assignment, transfer, and termination may be influenced by factors including:

- Skills and capabilities
- Knowledge and experience
- Organizational needs
- Relationships
- Trust
- Reputation
- Leadership preferences
- Organizational culture
- Visibility of work
- Informal leadership
- Political dynamics

As a result, organizations with similar structures may make different participation decisions under similar circumstances.

Formal organizational structures and recorded performance may not always fully represent actual contribution, influence, leadership, or organizational dependency.

Detailed participation decision modelling remains outside the current scope but should remain possible.
