# Principles

These principles guide the project’s public architecture, methodology, and domain documentation.

## Enterprise context before technology

Technology should be understood in the context of the enterprise purpose, capabilities, responsibilities, people, and operating environment it supports.

## Value- and business-driven modelling

Enterprise structures should arise coherently from intended value and business needs rather than being assembled as disconnected technical components.

## Concepts before implementations

Stable concepts should remain separate from changing products, vendors, configurations, and organizational procedures. Implementations may specialize the model without redefining its conceptual foundation.

## Clear architectural boundaries

Enterprise definition, generation, state, and simulation are different responsibilities. The simulation platform and learning experiences are also distinct concerns.

## Core Methodology with Domain Profiles

The Core Methodology provides a reusable delivery lifecycle. Domain Profiles extend that lifecycle with discipline-specific concepts, activities, evidence, risks, and examples. A profile must not replace or duplicate the core.

## Validate before transition

Design and implementation are not the end of delivery. Phase 06 validates the outcome against agreed needs and evidence. Phase 07 transitions the capability into sustainable operations with ownership, support, knowledge transfer, monitoring, and improvement mechanisms.

## Evidence before assertion

Claims, assessments, and AI-supported explanations should be grounded in traceable evidence. AI may assist analysis and explanation, but it should not invent facts or substitute confidence for validation.

## Determinism where it matters

Deterministic behaviour should be preferred where repeatability, testing, traceability, or comparison is important. Probabilistic or AI-assisted behaviour should be introduced deliberately and remain explainable.

## Cross-domain and multi-perspective learning

The same enterprise should be understandable from different professional perspectives. Documentation and scenarios should reveal how decisions and events propagate across organizational domains.

## Teach decisions, not only procedures

Material should explain why a decision exists, which needs and constraints shaped it, what alternatives were considered, and what consequences follow.

## Realism with explicit limits

The project aims for coherent and useful representations of enterprise behaviour, not a claim of complete fidelity. Assumptions, simplifications, coverage limits, and fictional data should be stated clearly.

## Modularity without premature complexity

The architecture should allow future extension without forcing speculative future capabilities into the current design. New domains and profiles should be added through clear boundaries.

## Privacy and safe examples

Public documentation and scenarios should use fictional or appropriately anonymized information. Private source, development history, operational secrets, customer information, and sensitive organizational detail do not belong in the public repository.
